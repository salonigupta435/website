Welcome

Education economist studying innovation and technology in India’s schools and colleges.

I am the Jonathan M. Nelson Assistant Professor of Entrepreneurship and Education at Brown University. Some of my current projects examine how college students learn to use generative AI to become experts, whether schools can teach innovation, how personalized adaptive learning performs at scale, and whether diversity initiatives help high-achieving women engineers land better jobs.

I received my Ph.D. in Economics and Education from Teachers College, Columbia University (2024) and was a postdoctoral fellow at the Stanford King Center on Global Development (2025).

I am an invited researcher at J-PAL’s Science for Progress Initiative and was a National Academy of Education/Spencer Dissertation Fellow (2023). My research has been funded by the Stanford King Center on Global Development, the Weiss Fund for Development Economics, the What Works Hub for Global Education, the Annenberg Institute, and The Agency Fund.

Recent Work

Can Schools Teach Innovation? Experimental Evidence from India

An innovation curriculum raised participation in an innovation competition by 8 percentage points, the quality of student ideas, and skills in exploration, idea generation, fluid intelligence, and problem solving.

Scaling Up Personalized Adaptive Learning in India

Personalized adaptive learning in government schools raised mathematics achievement by 0.39 SD over two years, about 1.8 additional years of learning.

Measuring Foundational Learning by Phone: Evidence from a Randomized Crossover Experiment

Children's literacy and numeracy scores by phone match their in-person scores skill by skill, and the pooled difference is a precisely estimated null.

Mother Tongue by Policy, English-Medium by Demand

English-medium enrolment in India rose from 5 to 29 percent of students between 2005-06 and 2025-26 and now dominates in major cities, even though the National Education Policy 2020 recommends teaching in the mother tongue until at least Grade 5.
